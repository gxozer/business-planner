# Research Checklist

Use this as a guide for what to look up, not a rigid script — skip areas that don't apply, and go deeper where the idea's viability actually hinges on the answer.

## 1. Market sizing

- **TAM** (Total Addressable Market): the total possible market if the product owned 100% of it.
- **SAM** (Serviceable Addressable Market): the slice reachable given the business's actual model, geography, and channels.
- **SOM** (Serviceable Obtainable Market): a realistic capture estimate over the plan's time horizon (e.g. 1–3 years), given competition and go-to-market limits.
- Source these top-down (industry reports, market research firms, government statistics) and, where possible, sanity-check bottom-up (number of target customers × realistic price × realistic penetration rate).
- Note the market's growth trend (shrinking, flat, growing — and how fast).

## 2. Competitive landscape

For each meaningful competitor (direct and indirect substitutes):

- What they offer, pricing, target segment.
- Apparent traction (funding raised, headcount, review volume/ratings, public revenue if available).
- Their likely weakness or gap the idea could exploit.
- Don't stop at "no direct competitors" — indirect substitutes (including "doing nothing" / manual workarounds) almost always exist and matter for adoption difficulty.

## 3. Customer / demand signals

- Evidence people actually want this: forum complaints, search trend data, existing willingness to pay for adjacent solutions, waitlists/surveys if the user has run any.
- Identify the likely early-adopter segment specifically, not just "everyone who has this problem."

## 4. Trends & regulation

- Relevant macro or industry trends (technology shifts, consumer behavior changes) that help or hurt over the plan horizon.
- Regulatory, licensing, or compliance requirements specific to the industry and geography (e.g. health, finance, food, data privacy) — flag anything that could be a hard blocker or meaningfully delay launch.

## 5. Pricing & unit economics benchmarks

- What comparable products charge, and by what model (subscription, one-time, usage-based, commission).
- Typical costs in the space: customer acquisition cost (CAC), gross margin, churn, if publicly known for comparable companies.

## 6. Patent & IP landscape

- Search Google Patents, USPTO, and WIPO (via `WebSearch`/`WebFetch`) for prior art covering the idea's core mechanism, method, or a distinctive technical feature — not the business model itself (business models are rarely patentable on their own).
- For each relevant prior-art hit: patent/application number, holder, filing/grant date, and how closely it overlaps.
- Note whether the overlap looks like a **freedom-to-operate risk** (something the business might infringe if built as described) versus just **adjacent art** (similar space, different mechanism).
- Flag anything that looks like a real blocker as prominently as a saturated market or a regulatory blocker — don't bury it.
- This is a landscape scan an AI assistant can do with web search, not a legal opinion. Always label it as such, and never state that something "is patentable" or "is clear to use" as a fact — describe what prior art was and wasn't found, and recommend a registered patent attorney or agent for an actual opinion before filing or relying on it.

## Source quality

- Prefer primary or high-quality secondary sources: government/statistical agencies, industry association reports, reputable market research firms, company financial filings, direct competitor pricing pages.
- Note the publication date of any figure — market data older than 2–3 years should be flagged as dated, not presented as current.
- When sources disagree, say so and give a range rather than picking one number silently.
- Always retain the source URL so it can go in the plan's "Sources" section.
